# Semantic Progression

## Sentence functions

Choose the function that advances the paragraph:

- name or define
- develop a cause, motive, mechanism, or relationship
- supply evidence, detail, image, or example
- create a consequence, realization, or change
- establish a boundary, qualification, or meaningful contrast
- move the argument, scene, or action

## Link audit

Make each sentence answer a live question raised by the text. Name the relationship between every adjacent pair: definition, elaboration, sequence, cause, motive, evidence, consequence, boundary, contrast, or action. Rewrite, reorder, or split when the relationship cannot be named.

Keep the same noun for the same subject. Change terms only when the meaning changes. Prefer verbs that reveal what the subject does.

## Necessity tests

- **Deletion:** remove the sentence. Keep it when understanding, expectation, emotion, or action loses something material.
- **Link:** identify its relationship with the preceding text. Repair unsupported jumps.
- **Substitution:** replace the subject with an unrelated one. Rewrite language that still appears to fit any topic.

Compress after the logic holds. Sentence length controls pace and emphasis; semantic progression controls signal.

## Rhetorical discipline

Use rhetoric to carry information. Reserve repeated syntax for deliberate rhythm.

- **Antithesis** balances one idea against another.
- **Epanorthosis** stages a correction or sharper reformulation.

Use either device when both sides contribute meaning. Rewrite recurring correction patterns as direct claims:

- `not X, but Y`
- `not only X, but also Y`
- `X did A; X did not do B`

Build rhythm from the material. Replace forced triads, generic emphasis, recap questions, strings of short declarations, and repeated punctuation with detail, movement, or consequence.

Use a rhetorical question when its answer opens necessary reasoning. State an established consequence directly.

## Examples

Choppy progression:

> The room went quiet. This mattered. Everyone understood.

Semantic progression:

> The sudden quiet told everyone the decision had been made.

Staged correction:

> The garden is not decoration; it is a record of care.

Direct claim:

> Years of care remain visible in the garden's surviving plants and repaired paths.
